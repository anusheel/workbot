# How we write

For prompts, documents, the course, commit messages and notes. The rules are the same; the length differs.

- Plain sentences, one idea each; short answers; numerals for numbers, with units and currency.
- Say the thing, not the feeling about it: no hyperbole, no slogans, no aphorisms, no "simply", no "powerful".
- Claims carry their source: a document title, a file path, a ticket, a measurement with its assumptions. A claim with none is cut or labelled a hypothesis.
- Name what a thing is before using the term; gloss jargon once.
- Checkable outcomes over promises: "prints 12 rows and the mean" rather than "works well".
- A prompt tells the model what to do and when to stop, in its own voice: "Numerals for numbers; no guesses."
- A commit subject is imperative and specific; the body says why when the diff does not. Ticket ids in the subject, never in the code.
- A document's first paragraph states the point; headings stand on their own; tables for anything with 3 columns of facts.
- No reference to a session, a tool run or a temporary file in anything committed.
